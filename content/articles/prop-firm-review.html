THE SHORT VERSION

A prop firm is a proprietary trading business. But an online “funded account” can be simulated, with rewards governed by a contract. Understanding what is actually being offered matters more than the advertised account size.

What is a prop firm?

A proprietary trading firm trades for its own account. In the traditional model, the firm supplies capital, sets risk limits and runs its trading operation. Traders work within that framework rather than simply investing their personal savings.

Online, “prop firm” is also used for businesses offering trading evaluations to individual participants. These services can differ significantly from a conventional trading desk. Some provide simulated accounts and pay rewards under a separate agreement. A familiar label does not mean every business follows the same model.

For example, FTMO's programme explanation describes simulated trading during evaluation and on its subsequent FTMO Account. That is useful context for interpreting the word “funded,” but it should not be treated as a description of every provider.

How proprietary trading works

Separate three questions: what resources are provided, which rules apply and how the trader is compensated. In a traditional proprietary operation, these form part of the working relationship with the firm. In an online evaluation, they are defined by the programme's terms.

Choose an account or evaluation

Start with the service you are actually purchasing. Is the payment for an evaluation, a subscription, platform access or something else? An advertised account size does not tell you how much you can withdraw, what is simulated or how much participation may cost.

Trade within defined limits

Read the rules before placing a trade. A daily loss limit, an overall loss limit and a profit objective describe different things. Calculations may depend on balance, equity including open positions, a fixed starting value or a moving reference point. Those details change the practical meaning of the headline numbers.

Understand the reward agreement

Passing an evaluation and receiving a payment are separate events. Check the conditions for requesting a reward, the verification process, the calculation of eligible results and the reasons a request may be rejected. A prominently displayed percentage is not a substitute for the complete payment policy.

Potential benefits and trade-offs

A structured environment can make trading rules explicit. Clear limits and a record of decisions may be useful reference points when reviewing your process. However, their value depends on the actual service and your goals. Structure alone is not evidence of future earnings.

The trade-off is accepting another party's rules. A method used on a personal account may conflict with a programme's restrictions. Investigate whether holding positions overnight, trading around scheduled news, using automation or copying trades is allowed. Never assume an activity is permitted because a different provider allows it.

Fees remain a real cost when trading is simulated.

In March 2024, Belgium's FSMA warned about consumer risks in certain prop trading models, including repeated challenge fees, demo accounts and uncertainty about compensation. Read that warning in its stated context; it is not a worldwide legal conclusion about every firm. Read the FSMA warning.

What to check before paying a fee

  • The contracting company. Identify the legal entity, contact details and complaints process.
  • The account type. Ask whether trading is simulated or live and what the displayed balance represents.
  • The complete cost. Include repeat attempts, resets, recurring charges and other disclosed fees.
  • The risk calculation. Look for worked examples of loss limits, including open positions.
  • The payment policy. Check eligibility, verification, processing periods and dispute procedures.
  • Rule changes. Keep a dated copy of the agreement and understand how updates are communicated.

Our review: clarity comes before account size

Our editorial view is that transparency should be the starting point. A useful explanation tells you what you are buying, how you can breach the rules and what must happen before a payment is due. If any answer is difficult to find, a larger advertised account does not resolve the uncertainty.

When reading other reviews, look for context rather than a single payout screenshot. Which programme was used? Which terms applied? What did participation cost? How were problems handled? These questions produce a more informative comparison than a headline score alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does passing a challenge guarantee a live account?

No. Read the specific agreement. Some programmes explicitly describe the account used after evaluation as simulated.

Can I withdraw the advertised account balance?

Do not infer withdrawal rights from the account size. A nominal balance and an eligible reward are separate concepts defined by the programme.

Is a prop firm the same as a broker?

The labels describe different roles. Identify the actual services and legal entity rather than assuming an evaluation offers the same relationship or protections as a brokerage account.

Are all programmes the same in 2026?

No universal set of terms applies. Check the current agreement for the specific programme, even when a recent review describes it differently.
